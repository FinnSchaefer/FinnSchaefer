First place at NTCC

This write-up covers the work behind my first-place finish in the National Transportation Cybersecurity Competition and why that result mattered beyond the competition itself.

NTCC pulled together the kind of problems I care most about: systems that are operational, time-sensitive, and difficult to reason about if you treat security as an isolated layer. The competition forced quick context switching across anomaly detection, application security, and exploitation in transportation-oriented systems.

What made the work valuable to me was not just the result. It showed that the way I like to approach security engineering, staying technical, calm, and grounded in how a system actually behaves, holds up when the environment is competitive and the constraints are real.

The anomaly-detection side stood out most because it sits close to where I want to keep working: at the point where machine learning, telemetry, and cyber-physical security start affecting operational decisions. That is the thread that connects this result to the rest of the work on the site.

I treat this write-up as a checkpoint. It marks a moment where transportation security, practical engineering, and system-level thinking all aligned in a way that clarified what kind of work I want to keep doing next.
