First place at NTCC

The NTCC first-place finish came out of a mix of different problem spaces, but the part that stuck with me most was the anomaly detection work on vehicle data.

The competition itself covered a wide range of challenges. Things like traffic system exploitation, web vulnerabilities, and classical crypto all showed up. But the more interesting problems were the ones where you had to reason about system behavior instead of just finding a bug.

For me, that meant working through vehicle trajectory data and figuring out where something did not line up. Not in an obvious way, but in a way that suggested the system's understanding of the environment was off. That is a different kind of problem than typical CTF-style work. It is less about breaking something directly and more about understanding how the system can be led into making the wrong decision.

That experience lined up pretty closely with the direction I have been moving in. It is the same underlying idea: systems trust their inputs more than they should, and if you can stay within what looks normal, you get a lot more room to operate.

Winning NTCC was not just about solving a set of challenges. It reinforced that this is the kind of problem space I want to keep working in. Not just finding vulnerabilities, but understanding how systems interpret data, and where that interpretation can be pushed off course.
