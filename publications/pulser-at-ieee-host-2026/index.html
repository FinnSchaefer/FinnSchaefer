LiDAR Spoofing for Compromising SLAM on ROS2

The HOST 2026 paper presents PULSER, a ROS2 LiDAR spoofing framework that manipulates SLAM inputs while preserving the timing and statistical shape of believable laser scans.

Paper
LiDAR Spoofing for Compromising SLAM on ROS2 Using Statistical Beam Adjustment & Temporal Modeling
Authors
Finn Schaefer, Ryan Lam, Sidarth Kumar, Jimmy Tram, Neil Sharma, and Md Tanvir Arafin
Venue
IEEE International Symposium on Hardware Oriented Security and Trust (HOST), 2026

Abstract

Autonomous robots rely on LiDAR-driven simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) to build and navigate through their environment. The paper studies how those pipelines behave when the sensor stream is not broken outright, but altered in a way that still looks plausible to ROS2 middleware and downstream SLAM components.

PULSER intercepts and overrides the ROS2 LiDAR topic, then injects structured distortions into the laser scan stream. The goal is to bias scan matching, corrupt the internal pose estimate, and create persistent map deformation without needing to modify the SLAM software stack itself.

\[ \tilde{r}_{i,t} = r_{i,t} + \delta_{i,t}, \qquad \delta_{i,t}\ \text{remains plausible across beams and time} \]

In experiments on simulated and physical platforms, low-magnitude range manipulation caused measurable localization drift and structural errors in the generated occupancy map. The result is a cyber-physical failure mode where the robot's map and pose can move away from reality while the incoming data continues to look usable.
